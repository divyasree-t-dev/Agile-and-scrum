# Online Grocery Delivery Application
### Day 3 – Agile and Scrum – Foundations and Backlog Workshop

## Project Overview

The Online Grocery Delivery Application is an Agile-based project designed for a regional supermarket chain. The application allows customers to browse grocery products, search for items, manage their shopping carts, place orders, make secure payments, and track deliveries.

This project focuses on preparing a prioritised and estimated Product Backlog using Scrum principles.

## Product Vision

To provide customers with a convenient and reliable online grocery shopping experience through an easy-to-use platform that simplifies product discovery, ordering, payment, and delivery tracking.

## Scrum Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Product Owner | Defines product goals and prioritises the backlog. |
| Scrum Master | Facilitates Scrum practices and removes impediments. |
| Developers | Design, develop, test, and deliver product increments. |

## Project Epics

The Product Backlog is organised into three major epics.

1. **Browse and Search** – Product browsing, searching, details, filtering, and sorting.
2. **Cart and Checkout** – Cart management, order placement, and secure payments.
3. **Delivery Tracking** – Order confirmation, delivery tracking, and notifications.

## Backlog Summary

| Epic | Stories | Story Points |
|---|---:|---:|
| Browse and Search | 4 | 16 |
| Cart and Checkout | 5 | 26 |
| Delivery Tracking | 3 | 10 |
| **Total** | **12** | **52** |

## Story Point Estimation

The Fibonacci sequence (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21) was used for relative effort estimation.

The reference story is **US01 – Browse Grocery Products**, estimated at 3 story points.

The complete backlog contains 12 user stories with a total estimate of 52 story points.

## JIRA Implementation

The project backlog will be maintained using a JIRA Scrum board.

The JIRA board will contain:

- Three Epics
- Twelve User Stories
- MoSCoW priorities
- Fibonacci story point estimates
- Sprint Backlog
- Sprint tracking

## Definition of Ready (DoR)

A story must have clear requirements, business value, acceptance criteria, priority, story points, and identified dependencies before entering a Sprint.

## Definition of Done (DoD)

A story must have completed implementation, code review, relevant testing, acceptance criteria validation, and required documentation before being considered Done.
